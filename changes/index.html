Changes and corrections

What we changed, and why

Science corrects itself, and so does this lab. When we find a mistake, we fix it and record it here: what it said, what it says now, and why. Results never change silently.

What never changes: the rules written before each experiment (the pre-registration files). They record what we decided before seeing any result, so they stay exactly as written, even when a page that describes them is corrected.

21 November 2026: full claims check

Every number on the lab was checked against its data files and recounted from the raw FlyWire data, one simulation was re-run independently (it reproduced exactly), and every cited paper was checked for what it actually showed. The counts all held. These are the things that needed fixing.

Number

"Clavicle": drinking drop is 83%, range 81 to 85%

Was: 84% (95% range 82 to 85%) in the notebook, the What's next page and the screen text.

Why: the true values are 83.496% and 81.48%. They had been rounded once when saved, and then rounded again for display, which pushed them up a point. The Discovery table had shown the right 83% (81 to 85%) all along, but earlier the same day it was wrongly changed to match the notebook. Now every page shows 83% (81 to 85%), and the saved values keep more decimal places so this can't happen again.

Pages: Neuron Silencing Screen, What's next, Lab notebook.

Was: "…and stopped drinking even of strong sugar (1 M)." Now: stopped proboscis (tongue) extension, even to strong sugar.

Citation

The name "MN9" came later than the 2009 paper

Was: "MN9 is a motor neuron that drives proboscis extension" (Gordon & Scott 2009). Now: a pair of sugar-activated motor neurons controls proboscis extension; later work named them MN9.

Page: Calibration test.

Citation

ppk28: what the paper showed

Was: "Water-sensing neurons (ppk28) drive drinking." Now: water-sensing taste neurons carry the water receptor ppk28; they respond to water, and flies without ppk28 drink less. The paper did not test whether switching them on makes flies drink.

Page: Calibration test. Source: Cameron et al. 2010.

Citation

Bitter neurons (Gr66a): avoidance

Was: "…does not, and suppresses extension." Now: activating bitter neurons drives avoidance instead.

Page: Calibration test. Source: Marella et al. 2006.

Citation

The 91% figure

Was: "91% of 164 predictions held in living flies." Now: of the 164 predictions the authors could test in living flies, 91% held.

Page: Calibration test. Source: Shiu et al. 2024.

Citation

Fudog also responds to sugar

Was: "Fudog responds to water." Now adds: it also responds to sugar. (The lab's water test of Fudog is unchanged.)

Pages: Lab notebook, experiments round 1 (E5).

Citation

PPL2 dopamine neurons: calyx as well as lateral horn

Was: "PPL2 reaches the lateral horn." Now: PPL2 was traced to the calyx (where smells enter the mushroom body) and to the lateral horn (Mao & Davis 2009). In the FlyWire wiring itself, nearly half of PPL2's synapses are in the lateral horn, so the data card now says "nearly half" instead of "mainly".
